\begin{abstract}
We derive the partial bound $2587/100000=0.02587$ for sparse halves
of every finite triangle-free graph. The external input is the
Balogh--Clemen--Lidick\'y max-cut theorem. We extend the completion
calculation in Razborov's Section 4.5 to independence ratio $1/3$,
and apply it after deleting the edges within one side of a cut.
All scalar certificates are rational. This draft is not a solution
of Erd\H{o}s problem 128, is not formalized in Lean, and makes no
claim of priority.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and normalization}
For $n>0$, let $\bh(G)$ be the minimum of
$n^{-2}\sum_{uv\in E(G)}z_uz_v$ over $0\le z_v\le1$ and
$\sum z_v=n/2$. A transfer between two partial coordinates has a
concave or affine cost. Successive transfers give a minimizer with
at most one partial coordinate. If $n$ is even it is integral; if
$n$ is odd, dropping its one coordinate equal to $1/2$ produces
exactly $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ selected vertices without increasing cost.
Also $\bh(G[t])=\bh(G)$: average a profile over each independent twin
class to obtain a profile on $G$, and conversely lift profiles.

\begin{theorem}
Assuming the published Balogh--Clemen--Lidick\'y bound
$D_2(G)\le 2n^2/47$, every finite triangle-free graph has a
$\lfloor n/2\rfloor$-vertex set spanning at most
$2587n^2/100000$ edges. For $n>0$ the displayed bound is strict.
\end{theorem}
For $n=0$ the empty set proves the non-strict statement. The target
$n^2/50$ remains unproved.

\section{An independent-set envelope}
Define, for $1/3\le x\le1/2$,
\[
 f(x)=\begin{cases}
  \frac32x^2-\frac54x+\frac9{32},&1/3\le x\le3/8,\\
  \frac{x}{2}(\frac12-x),&3/8\le x\le1/2.
 \end{cases}
\]
It is continuous and decreasing on this interval. Set $f(x)=0$
for $x\ge1/2$.

\begin{lemma}\label{ind}
If a triangle-free graph has an independent set of size at least
$xn$, where $1/3\le x\le1/2$, then $\bh(G)\le f(x)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We give the completion calculation, adapting Razborov
\cite[Section 4.5]{raz}. Write $\alpha$ for the maximum independent
set size divided by $n$. The case $\alpha\ge1/2$ is immediate.
It suffices to prove the result for $x=\alpha$; monotonicity then
applies. By doubling the graph we may assume $n$ even.

All vertex sizes and degrees below are divided by $n$; all edge
counts are divided by $n^2$ and count unordered edges once.
Start with a maximum independent set and add vertices of degree
into the current set at most $k=1/2-\alpha$, stopping on reaching
size $1/2$ or when none exists. Reaching a half costs at most
$k^2\le\alpha k/2\le f(\alpha)$, since $\alpha\ge1/3$.
Otherwise the resulting set $B$ has size $b\in[\alpha,1/2)$,
edge mass at most $k(b-\alpha)$, and every outside vertex has
degree into $B$ greater than $k$.

Vertices outside $B$ with degree into $B$ greater than $b/2$
form an independent set $C$, so $|C|/n\le\alpha$. Choose
$D$ outside $B\cup C$ of size $d=1-\alpha-b$. Every vertex
of $D$ has degree into $B$ in $(k,b/2]$. Put $h=1/2-b$.

If some vertex in $B$ has at least $hn$ neighbors in $D$, select
$hn$ of them. They are independent and complete $B$ to a half
with cost at most
\[
 H=k(b-\alpha)+bh/2.
\]
For $\alpha\le3/8$ the identity
\[
 H=\frac32\alpha^2-\frac54\alpha+\frac9{32}
   -\tfrac12(b-(3/4-\alpha))^2
\]
proves the claim. For $\alpha\ge3/8$, instead use
\[
 H=\alpha k/2+(3/4-2\alpha)(b-\alpha)
      -(b-\alpha)^2/2\le\alpha k/2.
\]

In the remaining case choose a uniformly random vertex $v$ of
$B$, put $E=N(v)\cap D$, and write $r=|E|/n<h$.
On $B\cup E$ assign weight one and on $D\setminus E$ assign
weight $(h-r)/(d-r)$; elsewhere assign zero. This is a fractional
half. Notice $d-h=k>0$, so the denominator is positive.
The set $E$ is independent. If $m(B,E)$ denotes its cross-edge
mass with $B$, the cost is at most
\[
 k(b-\alpha)+m(B,E)+\tfrac b2(h-r)
       +r(h-r)+(h-r)^2/4.
\]
The last term uses Mantel's inequality on $D\setminus E$.
Let $\bar r$ denote the mean of $r$. A vertex $z\in D$ of
degree $s_z$ into $B$ is selected with probability $s_z/b$.
Since $s_z^2\le(k+b/2)s_z-kb/2$, double counting gives
\[
 \mathbb E m(B,E)\le(k+b/2)\bar r-kd/2,
 \qquad 0\le\bar r\le d/2.
\]
The remaining expression is concave in $r$, so averaging bounds
the cost of some half by
\[
 Q=k(b-\alpha-d/2)+bh/2+h^2/4
                  +(k+h/2)\bar r-3\bar r^2/4.
\]
Its derivative at $\bar r=d/2$ is $(b-\alpha)/4\ge0$;
therefore it is increasing on $[0,d/2]$. Substitution yields
\[
 Q\le\frac{13}{16}\alpha^2-\frac98\alpha b
       -\frac3{16}b^2-\frac14\alpha+\frac12b
 =\frac{\alpha k}{2}
  +\frac{1-3\alpha}{2}(b-\alpha)-\frac3{16}(b-\alpha)^2
 \le\frac{\alpha k}{2}\le f(\alpha).
\]
This proves the lemma, including the endpoints.
\end{proof}

\section{Deleting and restoring one side of a cut}
Let $I=2/47$. The external max-cut theorem \cite{bcl} gives a
partition $X,Y$, $|X|\le|Y|$, whose internal edge masses $q,j$
satisfy $q+j\le I$. Its sufficiently-large-order formulation
extends to every positive order: the cut cost on twin classes is
separately affine, so $D_2(G[t])=t^2D_2(G)$, and then let $t$
be sufficiently large.

Set $c=n/(2|Y|)\in[1/2,1]$ and $x=|X|/n=1-1/(2c)$.
The profile $c\mathbf1_Y$ gives $\bh(G)\le c^2j$.
When $c\ge3/4$, delete the edges inside $X$. In the resulting
triangle-free graph, $X$ is independent and $x\ge1/3$.
Lemma \ref{ind}, followed by restoring the deleted edges, gives
$\bh(G)\le f(x)+q$. Consequently
\[
 \bh(G)\le Ic^2,
 \qquad
 \bh(G)\le\frac{c^2}{1+c^2}(I+f(x))\quad(c\ge3/4).
\]
For the second inequality, add the first profile bound and
$c^2$ times the deletion bound, and use $q+j\le I$.

\section{Exact scalar certificate}
Put $T=2587/100000$ and $c_0=7797/10000$.
For $c\le c_0$, $\bh(G)\le Ic_0^2<T$, with margin
$1291/2350000000$.

For $c_0\le c\le4/5$, the numerator of
$T-c^2(I+f(x))/(1+c^2)$ is
\[
 P(c)=(T-I-17/32)c^2+7c/8+T-3/8.
\]
It is concave, so its minimum on the interval is at an endpoint.
Both endpoints are positive:
\[
 P(c_0)=\frac{312483613}{235000000000000},\qquad
 P(4/5)=\frac{22649}{117500000}.
\]
For $4/5\le c\le1$ the numerator is $Ac^2-Bc+C$, where
\[
 A=T+1/4-I>0,\quad B=3/8,\quad C=T+1/8,
 \qquad 4AC-B^2=\frac{442569}{2500000000}>0.
\]
Completing the square proves positivity on the whole real line.
These three intervals exhaust $[1/2,1]$. Rounding the fractional
half proves the theorem for even and odd orders.

\section{Verification and limitations}
Run \texttt{python outputs/verify-cut-independent.py}. It uses
only integer and rational arithmetic. It checks all scalar margins
and polynomial identities independently and tests the extended
lemma on explicit weighted triangle-free graphs. It also enumerates
small labeled graphs to test the floor convention and $n=0$.
The finite tests are corroboration, not a proof of universality.
The universal argument is the proof above, with the external
max-cut theorem explicitly assumed. No Lean certificate of this
argument has been produced. No proof claim has been submitted.
